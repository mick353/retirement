Purpose and boundaries

What this page documents

This reference makes the model intelligible to people, accessibility tools, search engines and AI retrieval systems that do not execute React. All substantive content is present in this HTML response; JavaScript is not required.

Read the spending and capital checks together

Start with the V23 Decision Brief. In Plan controls, choose your target age and minimum total investments. You can add a later age and a separate essentials budget. Each spending check covers every year from retirement up to the selected birthday. Investments are measured on that birthday and exclude your home. The joint result counts market histories that meet both goals. The later check uses the same histories. Open V23 Decision Brief

Use Compare alternatives to put your plan, another flat budget, a pension election and a Pool C rule on the same 200 market paths. When changing elections, choose how to carry the budget. Inspect an age to follow pension spent, withdrawals, savings, shortfall and closing investments. The CSV gives the same cash flow at cent precision.

Command Centre shows its checks in Scenario → Risk. Select the option to carry a goal during handoff if you want both workspaces to use it. Export settings previews the plan-only file, including any manual figures used in the plan. Choose the full backup to include saved comparisons and personal review records. A matching plan ID checks the inputs and the version identifies the calculation code.

Surface compares both withdrawal rules. Applying spending and return in Command Centre keeps its reserve rule. The Pool C draft opens in V23 for an explicit choice. Returning from V23 previews the first-year budget as a flat comparison. The detailed schedule and provider assumptions remain visible in V23.

For a retirement one-off, distinguish the investment-floor-only maximum from the limit that also funds essentials. In Annual review, capture a dated forecast and then record actuals. Each recorded year can show its frozen forecast, observed spending and current revised forecast separately.

In Scenario → Lab, open First retirement year and fund payment dates to see living costs, estimated PSS payments and the ABP minimum through the following June as separate figures. Enter a dated provider payday and balance when available. The illustration uses the rules as at 1 July 2026. Schedule 7 · CSC paydays

The AI panels preview the selected data before sending. Your message and active model are included. Saved reviews, personal notes and recent chat are separate choices and start off.

Decision-support only. This is a personal planning model, not licensed financial, tax or legal advice. Future returns, longevity, legislation, PSS outcomes and provider implementation are uncertain. Reconfirm current rules and obtain professional advice before acting.

Start here: use and read the plan

  1. In Command Centre, check the active rail, dated CSC source basis and PSS election. Set one flat annual net spending amount and an after-inflation return assumption.
  2. In Scenario → Lab, compare the amount you want to spend with the amount the model funds. A shortfall is not extra income. Read the annual ledger and investment capital at your selected age together.
  3. In Scenario → Explore, inspect a chosen age in Income by year or the exact table. Use Compare and Frontier for spending trade-offs; use Risk for sampled and stress sensitivities, not personal forecast odds.
  4. In V23 Income & draws, set spending gaps by age if a flat amount does not describe your plan. On Guardrail spending, advice is the default; the opt-in adaptive comparison requires an explicit essential total budget and leaves the saved plan unchanged.
  5. Before a real decision, check the active source date and the limits in this reference, refresh actual balances and confirm current provider and tax rules.

Three amounts to keep separate: desired spending is your target; funded spending is what the selected pool policy supplies; investment capital is the remaining Pool A+B+C balance. A higher capital balance can result from spending less. Gross estate may add the selected home value and is not an after-tax inheritance estimate.

Scenario-aware early-retirement sensitivity

Voluntary retirement / redundancy at ages 57–59

The active Rail B provider basis and pension/lump election now supply the VR page's source-backed age-60 anchor. Both bases support 60/40, 65/35, 70/30 and 100% pension. Rail A remains available as the March/V5 historical 60/40 control.

Source boundary. The supplied September iEstimator PDFs provide age-60 elections, not formal redundancy estimates at 57, 58 or 59. Every earlier-age result is therefore labelled illustrative and must be replaced by a formal CSC estimate before a decision.

Provider CPIThe selected provider basis supplies 2.5% or 3% CPI and that assumption is already reflected in the CPI-reduced, today-dollar age-60 source values. It is not applied again by the VR illustration.
Real-dollar bridgeThe CPI-indexed PSS pension stays flat in real terms. The selected site real return alone grows the illustrated PSS lump and 48-week VR cash assumption to age 60. Existing Hostplus is shown separately.

At age 60, every VR path reconciles exactly to the selected provider pension and lump. Calculation layer

Deterministic projection and annual cash ledger

The Command Centre derives its spending frontier by re-running the operational annual planning ledger for each selected spending, return and target-age combination. It does not use a separate terminal monthly-draw formula. The selected home value is added only for a clearly labelled gross-estate view; fixed 4% and 6.5% paths are explicit comparisons.

Operational annual ledger

mandatoryDraw = openingPoolA × ageBasedMinimumRate
lifestyleGap  = max(0, targetTotalSpending - netPssPension)
actualDraw    = min(openingPoolA, max(mandatoryDraw, lifestyleGap))
netIncome     = netPssPension + actualDraw
fundedSpend   = min(targetTotalSpending, netIncome)
shortfall     = max(0, targetTotalSpending - netIncome)
reinvestment  = max(0, netIncome - targetTotalSpending)
poolCDrag     = openingPoolC × 0.0035
closingPoolA  = max(0, openingPoolA × (1 + realReturn) - actualDraw)
closingPoolC  = max(0, openingPoolC × (1 + realReturn) - poolCDrag + reinvestment)
100% pension is fully modelled. At $90k, $100k, $110k or $130k total net spending, voluntary portfolio draw is $0 and the annual PSS surplus routed to Pool C is respectively $53,104.26, $43,104.26, $33,104.26 or $13,104.26.

Birthday-year planning rows apply the rate for age at the preceding 1 July: under 65, 4%; 65–74, 5%; 75–79, 6%; 80–84, 7%; 85–89, 9%; 90–94, 11%; 95+, 14%. This prevents an age threshold being stepped up early. The provider calculates the actual financial-year minimum, including first-year pro-rating; Pool C is an invested ETF reserve, not cash and not an automatic source of a displayed shortfall.

V23 deterministic governor

  1. Apply the age-based mandatory draw from Pool A.
  2. Interpret a shared Command Centre target as total net spending. V23 derives the portfolio gap or PSS surplus.
  3. Draw extra from Pool A only when the spending gap exceeds the mandatory draw.
  4. Route surplus to available super headroom when permitted, otherwise to Pool C.
  5. Keep Pool B as a transit account for wash/recontribution modelling rather than a permanent growth pool.

V23 age bands let you set the whole after-tax budget, extra dollars above the net pension, a share of positive investment earnings or a share of the mandatory draw. Each band maps to its own lifestyle phase. A phase subdivides when its funding changes.

Pool C stays invested by default. The full-draw option pays remaining spending from Pool C after Pools A and B. The capped option allows withdrawals up to the current balance minus the selected Pool C reserve, with a minimum withdrawal capacity of zero. The separate one-off switch applies this cap to one-offs and illustrative care costs. Market losses can reduce the balance below the reserve.

The static reference describes the algorithm. The interactive V23 page remains the authoritative surface for changing profile-specific floor, target, strong-estate and spending-governor settings.

Uncertainty layer

Monte Carlo, sequence and shock tests

Integrated Command Centre fan

  • 600 reproducible seeded paths from age 60 through age 95.
  • Annual returns are sampled from a capped normal distribution with the selected real mean and volatility, clamped between −55% and +45%.
  • Each sampled return path runs the annual three-pool ledger: pension income, funded spending or shortfall, mandatory draw, investment growth, Pool C drag and surplus routing remain distinct.
  • The chart reports P10, P25, P50, P75 and P90 balance paths plus a model success frequency. These are simplified stress-test outputs, not forecast probabilities or guarantees.

V23 stochastic lab

The Command Centre and V23 run seeded samples, so unchanged inputs reproduce the same output. V23 exposes run count, expected real return, volatility, target age and guardrail thresholds; deliberately choose a new seed to inspect a different sample. They report sampled frequencies of clearing selected investment-capital bands, not forecast probabilities. The simulations use the modelled pool routing but do not model future legislation, actual provider financial-year payment timing, market regimes or personal spending shocks. Treat the return assumption as net of product fees.

Sequence-risk isolation

The sequence test uses one constructed set of annual returns in best-first and worst-first order; their difference isolates ordering within this model. The smooth line is a separate constant-mean reference, not the same returns in another order.

Stress controls

The one-off shock path applies the selected shock return at the chosen age and the mean return in other years. V23 also includes a CPI-lag stress over the selected shock duration: the model lowers real pension value during that window and applies the chosen real-return drag, so invested assets may be drawn to help fund the gap.

Estate and super sequencing

NCC wash and death-benefit-tax methods

The planning objective is to reduce the PSS taxable component exposed to an illustrative 17% adult non-tax-dependant death-benefit-tax rate, while respecting contribution eligibility, TSB/TBC headroom and transaction sequencing. The engine now uses the exact components from the active iEstimator and never assigns the PSS ratio to unresolved Hostplus components.

Modelled sequence

  2. Commute the selected amount from Pool A under the chosen component method.

Comparators

Tax, salary equivalence, spending and estate

Salary-equivalent comparator

The dashboard compares retirement cash flow with employment salary by calculating resident individual income tax plus a 2% Medicare levy, then solving by binary search for the gross salary that produces the selected net income. The baseline tax function uses a $18,200 tax-free threshold, bracket points at $45,000, $135,000 and $190,000, rates of 30%, 37% and 45% above those points, and a lower bracket rate of 15% for 2026–27 or 14% for 2027–28.

This deliberately simplified comparator excludes offsets, Medicare levy variations, Medicare levy surcharge, HELP, deductions and household-specific effects. It is a modelled comparison, not a tax return estimate.

Spending guardrails

A shared Command Centre budget specifies total net spending. V23 derives the amount needed from investments or the pension surplus. Set a changing budget in V23 Income & draws using the age bands.

V23 Adaptive Policy Comparator

On V23’s Guardrail spending page, advisory instructions remain the default. The opt-in comparator runs the selected spending plan beside an adaptive version. The adaptive decision uses opening investment balances before that year’s return and adjusts only desired spending above an explicitly entered essential total budget. Changed spending feeds through the existing annual ledger into future balances. The comparison does not change the saved plan.

Choose whether to hold total lifestyle spending or the additional gap above PSS constant across elections. Both policies use the same deterministic assumptions and each Monte Carlo pair uses the same sampled return path. Age-75 and age-85 investment checks are informational unless a preferred or hard amount is explicitly entered. Investment capital excludes the home. The comparator does not rank after-tax estate or infer a liquid reserve from invested assets. Percentage-of-current-return spending is excluded from adaptive runs because it depends on a return unknown at the opening decision.

The funded age 60–69 column sums ten spending years ending at ages 61–70; funded and discretionary age 60–74 sum fifteen years ending at ages 61–75. Age 60 itself is the opening capital snapshot with no spending. Each total is cumulative, in today's dollars.

Estate view

The simplified frontier reports gross estate = projected investments + selected home value. V23 separately tracks account components, external assets and illustrative death-benefit tax so gross and after-tax outcomes are not conflated.

Audit controls

Known mismatches and model governance

Do not mix rails, elections or projection bases silently.
Each output and shared link carries Rail A or Rail B, the selected 60/40, 65/35, 70/30 or 100% election, and the verified provider basis.
Keep each provider estimate source-specific.
Both bases have four direct CSC estimates. The prudent 100% estimate is a later 9 September snapshot with FAS $162,485.36; its values are not interpolated from the other basis or the 1 September prudent files.
Do not invent Hostplus components.
Each PSS source uses its exact own component split. Hostplus remains unresolved rather than borrowing a PSS ratio.
Keep gross, net and salary-equivalent values distinct.
Gross pension, modelled net pension, net spending and gross salary equivalence are separate fields.
Keep real and nominal values distinct.
Retirement projections use real dollars. Pre-age-60 compounding is separately labelled nominal.
Preserve method labels.
Corrected proportioning, workbook reconciliation and full-conversion comparison are not interchangeable NCC wash methods.
Do not silently fund spending from Pool C.
A target-spend shortfall remains visible until an explicit Pool C draw policy is chosen and modelled.
Keep salary evidence in order.
Payroll/CSC history supports the corrected $138,394 birthday super salary; the September estimates prospectively start from the $143,099 current payslip salary. Statement 2026 remains pending correction/reissue and does not replace the September estimates.
Keep the Hostplus anchor honest.
$317,447.66 is an upper planning anchor. The governed workbook reconciles to $289,620.63 at 8.0% nominal under its documented timing and contribution assumptions.
Review annually.
PSS estimates, balances, tax rates, Medicare, draw rates, contribution caps, TBC/TSB rules and provider processes can change.

Machine-analysis contract

When analysing this model, an automated system should: distinguish documented inputs from modelled outputs; state the rail, PSS election, provider projection basis and wash source; keep the pre-retirement provider basis separate from the post-retirement real return; identify total spending versus portfolio gap or PSS surplus; never infer that a simulated percentile is a forecast; and never convert this documentation into personal financial advice.
