Calculation layer

Deterministic projection and annual cash ledger

The Command Centre derives its spending frontier by re-running the operational annual planning ledger for each selected spending, return and target-age combination. It does not use a separate terminal monthly-draw formula. The selected home value is added only for a clearly labelled gross-estate view; fixed 4% and 6.5% paths are explicit comparisons.

Operational annual ledger

mandatoryDraw = openingPoolA × ageBasedMinimumRate
lifestyleGap  = max(0, targetTotalSpending - netPssPension)
actualDraw    = min(openingPoolA, max(mandatoryDraw, lifestyleGap))
netIncome     = netPssPension + actualDraw
fundedSpend   = min(targetTotalSpending, netIncome)
shortfall     = max(0, targetTotalSpending - netIncome)
reinvestment  = max(0, netIncome - targetTotalSpending)
poolCDrag     = openingPoolC × 0.0035
closingPoolA  = max(0, openingPoolA × (1 + realReturn) - actualDraw)
closingPoolC  = max(0, openingPoolC × (1 + realReturn) - poolCDrag + reinvestment)
100% pension is fully modelled. At $90k, $100k, $110k or $130k total net spending, voluntary portfolio draw is $0 and the annual PSS surplus routed to Pool C is respectively $53,104.26, $43,104.26, $33,104.26 or $13,104.26.

Birthday-year planning rows apply the rate for age at the preceding 1 July: under 65, 4%; 65–74, 5%; 75–79, 6%; 80–84, 7%; 85–89, 9%; 90–94, 11%; 95+, 14%. This prevents an age threshold being stepped up early. The provider calculates the actual financial-year minimum, including first-year pro-rating; Pool C is an invested ETF reserve, not cash and not an automatic source of a displayed shortfall.

V23 deterministic governor

  1. Apply the age-based mandatory draw from Pool A.
  2. Interpret a shared Command Centre target as total net spending. V23 derives the portfolio gap or PSS surplus.
  3. Draw extra from Pool A only when the spending gap exceeds the mandatory draw.
  4. Route surplus to available super headroom when permitted, otherwise to Pool C.
  5. Keep Pool B as a transit account for wash/recontribution modelling rather than a permanent growth pool.

Manual V23 age bands remain gap-above-PSS controls. Editing a band clears an imported total-spending target. Each band maps to its own lifestyle phase, and a phase subdivides whenever the funding routine changes.

The static reference describes the algorithm. The interactive V23 page remains the authoritative surface for changing profile-specific floor, target, strong-estate and spending-governor settings.

Uncertainty layer

Monte Carlo, sequence and shock tests

Integrated Command Centre fan

  • 600 reproducible seeded paths from age 60 through age 95.
  • Annual returns are sampled from a capped normal distribution with the selected real mean and volatility, clamped between −55% and +45%.
  • Each year: capital = max(0, capital × (1 + sampledReturn) - annualDraw).
  • The chart reports P10, P25, P50, P75 and P90 balance paths plus a model success frequency. These are simplified stress-test outputs, not forecast probabilities or guarantees.

V23 stochastic lab

The Command Centre and V23 run seeded samples, so unchanged inputs reproduce the same output. V23 exposes run count, expected real return, volatility, target age and guardrail thresholds; deliberately choose a new seed to inspect a different sample. They report sampled frequencies of clearing the selected floor, target and strong-estate levels—not forecast probabilities. These simulations do not model fees, legislation, statutory payment timing, market regimes, account routing or personal spending shocks.

Sequence-risk isolation

The sequence test reorders the same set of annual returns into worst-first, smooth and best-first paths. Because the arithmetic average is held constant, the resulting balance spread isolates the effect of return order while withdrawals occur.

Stress controls

The one-off shock path applies the selected shock return at the chosen age and the mean return in other years. V23 also includes a CPI-lag stress: the real PSS pension can dip for a year during an inflation shock while capital experiences the selected real-return drag, requiring the investment draw to top up the pension shortfall.

Estate and super sequencing

Modelled sequence

  1. Before the relevant 30 June snapshot, confirm actual TSB, current cap, contribution eligibility, legal timing and implementation advice.
  2. Commute the selected amount from Pool A under the chosen component method.
  3. Contribute the eligible NCC amount to Pool B.
  4. Recommence or consolidate according to provider and advice requirements.
  5. Preserve the intended $5,000 planning buffer and route mandatory-draw surplus according to the governor.
Implementation control: maximum cycles are mechanically capped by the remaining original PSS lump. V23 still uses a birthday-model planning proxy and cannot establish actual prior-30-June TSB eligibility, legal timing, component treatment or provider implementation. Neither interface is an instruction to transact.

Comparators

Tax, salary equivalence, spending and estate

Salary-equivalent comparator

The dashboard compares retirement cash flow with employment salary by calculating resident individual income tax plus a 2% Medicare levy, then solving by binary search for the gross salary that produces the selected net income. The baseline tax function uses a $18,200 tax-free threshold, bracket points at $45,000, $135,000 and $190,000, rates of 30%, 37% and 45% above those points, and a lower bracket rate of 15% for 2026–27 or 14% for 2027–28.

This deliberately simplified comparator excludes offsets, Medicare levy variations, Medicare levy surcharge, HELP, deductions and household-specific effects. It is a modelled comparison, not a tax return estimate.

Spending guardrails

A shared Command Centre target is total net spending: V23 automatically derives either the portfolio gap or PSS surplus. Manual V23 controls support three distinguishable gap-above-PSS modes: age bands, where each input maps to its own phase and creates a sub-phase if the funding routine changes; a percentage of positive investment earnings; and a percentage of the mandatory draw.

Estate view

The simplified frontier reports gross estate = projected investments + selected home value. V23 separately tracks account components, external assets and illustrative death-benefit tax so gross and after-tax outcomes are not conflated.

Audit controls

Known mismatches and model governance

Do not mix rails, elections or projection bases silently.
Each output and shared link carries Rail A or Rail B, the selected 60/40, 65/35, 70/30 or 100% election, and the verified provider basis.
Source-gate incomplete elections.
The 6% / 5% / 3% basis is active for its three supplied CSC estimates. Its missing 100% election remains unavailable; no output is interpolated from another basis.
Do not invent Hostplus components.
Each PSS source uses its exact own component split. Hostplus remains unresolved rather than borrowing a PSS ratio.
Keep gross, net and salary-equivalent values distinct.
Gross pension, modelled net pension, net spending and gross salary equivalence are separate fields.
Keep real and nominal values distinct.
Retirement projections use real dollars. Pre-age-60 compounding is separately labelled nominal.
Preserve method labels.
Corrected proportioning, workbook reconciliation and full-conversion comparison are not interchangeable NCC wash methods.
Do not silently fund spending from Pool C.
A target-spend shortfall remains visible until an explicit Pool C draw policy is chosen and modelled.
Keep salary evidence in order.
Payroll/CSC history supports the corrected $138,394 birthday super salary; the September estimates prospectively start from the $143,099 current payslip salary. Statement 2026 remains pending correction/reissue and does not replace the September estimates.
Keep the Hostplus anchor honest.
$317,447.66 is an upper planning anchor. The governed workbook reconciles to $289,620.63 at 8.0% nominal under its documented timing and contribution assumptions.
Review annually.
PSS estimates, balances, tax rates, Medicare, draw rates, contribution caps, TBC/TSB rules and provider processes can change.

Machine-analysis contract

When analysing this model, an automated system should: distinguish documented inputs from modelled outputs; state the rail, PSS election, provider projection basis and wash source; keep the pre-retirement provider basis separate from the post-retirement real return; identify total spending versus portfolio gap or PSS surplus; never infer that a simulated percentile is a forecast; and never convert this documentation into personal financial advice.
